The lake was as forgotten as the souls who inhabited it.

Life was not found there. No fish swam. No toads sat along its lily padded edges. Not even a cricket to chirp in the night.

As years passed over the lake, silence overwhelmed it until sound itself seemed to forget the waters existence.

This however, is not to say the lake wasn't populated.

Those who, on black nights wondered into its cool water, could not be numbered among the living.

Rather, it was only those silent and forgotten souls whose lives had been undone by their own hands, who knew the lake.

I cannot say how they found the lake, I do not know.

Whether it was a purgatory, an unfortunate roaming, or guided by some hand who ferried them to that place, the result was the same.

They would come, emerging from the surrounding woods. And they would walk. Advancing slowly as if taking in the surface and world around them, knowing they'd not see it again. And they would walk into the waters.

Each step taking them further and further until they were fully submerged. Then the waters would go still, and only again be disturbed when some vast stretch of time would pass and some other hollowed soul would descend into the lake.

Payne had been one of these. A forgotten person in life, swallowed up by cares she could not overcome. Whether that be her mother, who had named her out of the bile that sat in her own heart. Or her older sister, who had sat happily and greedily under her mother's thumb all through their childhood. And had made sure to let Payne know the burden she constantly placed on her mother by performing whatever sin and travesty Payne had apparently committed that day. Or whether it was Payne herself. Constantly wondering at her own mind and thoughts. Doubting their validity in the face of condescension of her mother and sister.

She was nearly silent as a child, not wanting to disrupt the house. She went through school as a shadow, never engaging with other students, who seemed more than happy to ignore her entirely. And even as a young woman she only ever involved herself with the world outside of her mother's house when absolutely necessary.

This of course led her to an isolated and forgotten existence. And she did her best to keep it that way.

So it came as a surprise to all when one sunny afternoon noon Payne sat in a cafe reading, and up came a young man by the name of Lloyd. A charming fellow, bright eyed and full of life, if not a silent type himself. He'd come to the cafe before and had seen her there, sipping her tea and reading her books. Her soft black hair usually draped over her face as if it were a curtain to block out the world around her.

But her hair was up the day he approached her. He'd taken it as a sign of her openness. And whether she'd consciously been a little more open that day or not, she'd certainly opened up when he came and stood in front of her. He'd introduced himself and asked if she minded company. She hesitantly accepted, never before having had such an interaction. They talked and she found herself being drawn to the stranger, speaking more than she had in any time she could remember.

Time passed and they'd made arrangements to see each other again. And they did. Eventually a delicate love like daisies blossomed between them and they'd grown comfortable with each other.

Paynes mother and sister of course protested the boy. Always finding reason to belittle paynes choices. But Payne stood firm in her affection for Lloyd. Perhaps for the first time in her life, she didn't doubt herself.

Lloyd was a good man, soft and caring, yet firm in his desires and responsibilities. And Payne loved him with a love that she'd not known for another person before him, or after.

And after came sooner than love would have allowed. After came with a knock at paynes front door. A somber face greeted her.

"Madam, I have unfortunate news concerning you" said the policeman.

"Your fiancé Lloyd Williams was involved in an automobile accident tonight"

Paynes ears rang as her hearing dulled. She felt faint and had to hold onto the doorframe to steady herself.

"I'm afraid he has passed" said the man.

Payne did not scream when she heard these words. She did not gasp, she did not faint or cry. She said

"Ok, thank you sir"

And she closed the door.

Her mother was standing a little ways behind her, and asked who was at the door. Payne told her, and repeated the officers words. Her mother did however gasp, her eyes filled with tears as she collapsed onto her knees and cried. She'd not shown any amount of affection towards Lloyd before, and Payne felt no pity towards her at that moment. Payne didn't feel much of anything at that moment though. She simply walked past her mother and went up the stairs into her room. Closing the door behind her. Lying down on her bed, the grief came to her. And she tried to stiffen the sobs with her pillow as the already dark night seemed to darken even more around her.

She lay in that room for days. Her mother at first bringing her food, and then seeming to grow tired of Paynes grief, eventually stopped. Her sister only making appearances to tell her about how life moves on and she can't stay in her room forever.

Payne hardly noticed them though. To her, life seemed to have stopped. And the outside world, which had meant little to her before, now felt like a dream which was forgotten shortly after waking.

All forgotten except for Lloyd. Except for the love which she had so openly and foolishly given herself to. The days spent with him. Them holding hands visiting shops and cafes. Or sitting with each other by the fireplace not speaking yet being closer than words could have brought them. Or the warm afternoons they'd spent lying in the grass of the park, looking at the soft white clouds lazily floating against the blue skies. A bright blue sky shined just outside of Paynes window. But to her, even the blue sky was grey.

It was the smell of her own room that eventually forced her out. She'd not so much at washed her face for many days. And even in the grips of her heartache, she could could not take the smell of the grief filled room anymore.

It took all she had left for her to will her feet out of the house and to carry her back to the cafe where she'd first met Lloyd. And once there she sat in the same seat where he'd first approached her. But this time, he'd not be coming up to her. There'd be no bright smile from him, no warm conversation to fill the air. No fiery embers of a love that bow only existed in memory.

These thoughts felt like iron in her chest, cold and heavy. And they pulled on her chest so that she had to bury her face in her arms on the table to keep anyone from seeing the tears that broke free from her eyes.

The cafe worker woke Payne up, it was dark outside and Payne had fallen asleep with her head in her arms.

The worker politely told her that the cafe was closing and she'd have to leave. So Payne got up on unwilling feet and began the walk home. The evening was cool and the streets were empty. The night had brought children and parents, couples, lovers, and friends indoors to suppers around large tables. But Payne walked alone in the dark, her path illuminated by only the lanterns that lined the streets.

She wondered around in the night, not wanting to go home and face her mother and sister, who would surely have lots of complaints about her disappearing all afternoon. Though if they'd been angry or even if they'd yelled at her, she wouldn't have cared. Every voice, every step, every thought felt to her as if it were hidden behind a fog. Grief had swallowed her up and she found herself deep in its stomach.

The streets lanterns stretched ahead of her and rose up high on a bridge that passed over a black river.

She didn't intend to stop there, but the iron in her chest weighed her down and she felt there was no other choice but to stop for a moment. Up high on the bridge she peered over its edge to the river far below. And almost without even thinking about it, she pushed herself over. The fall was brief, and there were no thoughts of regret. Except for her time with Lloyd, she'd had no life before and it seemed only right that she should let go of the hope for one now. And so her fall was welcomed by her, and she only hoped that the next life would be more welcoming than this one.

Payne didn't understand where she was. She vividly remembered jumping. She remembered the cafe before. she remembered the anguish that had overtaken her since Lloyd's death. In fact, she remembered everything with more clairity than there had been when she'd experienced those things. As if she'd been stuck in a fog her whole life, and only now had the air cleared. This was not a welcomed clarity however. This new clairity was harsh and bitter. There was now no filter for her to pull her own thoughts through. Nothing to soften the blow of reality. And the reality was that Lloyd had died, and she'd jumped. She'd jumped and hit the black water and sank deep below, swallowing broken gasps of cold water as she'd went. And then she was walking. Walking in this black forest. It seemed to her that the trees themselves were made of shadows. And then on top of the grief that had been so brutally inflicted upon her, she now felt fear. Paynes mind began to race, she wanted out, she wanted to go home. But she would not, could not. Could not, the thought dawning on her that she was walking and she didn't want to be. She couldn't stop though. Payne tried to will her feet to quit moving, she tried to jump or fall down or anything at all just as long as she'd stop walking. But she could not.

The tress began to thin and her feet carried her unwillingly to a clearing. A large circle bathed in dim moonlight as it shone behind clouds. A dark lake lay in front of her. Beautiful at first, but the water was as black as tar, and as motionless too.
